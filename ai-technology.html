AI systems hub: choose the question first

Orientation and reading path

Modern AI is not a single product or a single model. It is a system of data, model behaviour, computing infrastructure, interfaces and people who decide where output may be used. This hub helps readers separate those layers and choose the ASARK guide that matches the question they have.

Models can recognise patterns, generate material or rank options. Agents add tools, memory and a sequence of actions around a model. Inference is the moment a trained model is used for a task; deployment is the work of connecting it to permissions, data, monitoring and a real workflow. An impressive demonstration alone does not show that a system is reliable or appropriate for a decision.

A practical lifecycle

Useful AI work begins by defining a task and the consequence of a wrong answer. Teams prepare data, select or adapt a model, test representative cases, deploy it with clear boundaries, and monitor failures or changed conditions. Feedback can improve a system, but it can also repeat poor assumptions when it is not reviewed carefully.

Limits are part of the system

AI can produce plausible output that is incomplete, biased or unsupported. Data quality, latency, energy use, privacy and human review shape what a system can responsibly do. For high-consequence work, explanation, escalation and correction matter as much as capability.
